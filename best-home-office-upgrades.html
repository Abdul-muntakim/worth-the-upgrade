Best Home-Office Upgrades: What to Buy First

A sensible order for improving a UK home office without buying everything at once.

Updated 24 September 2026·Practical UK guide
Quick answer

Start with fit and comfort: chair first, then desk height, then monitor position and only then optional accessories.

1. Fix the chair and desk relationship first

Your feet should be supported, elbows should sit near desk height and the screen should not force the neck downward. An adjustable chair usually creates the biggest immediate change because it affects every seated hour.

  • Measure seat height and desk clearance
  • Check lumbar support against your natural posture
  • Prioritise return terms because chair fit is personal

2. Add sit–stand flexibility, not an all-day standing target

A standing desk is useful because it makes changing position easier. It is not a reason to stand still for eight hours. Use memory presets and switch between sitting, standing and short walks.

  • Choose a height range that fits you seated and standing
  • Allow enough desktop depth for a comfortable screen distance
  • Plan cable length before raising the desk

3. Position the screen and input devices

The top portion of the display should be around eye level for many users, while the keyboard and mouse should allow relaxed shoulders. A laptop used for long periods benefits from an external keyboard, mouse and raised screen.

4. Treat air care as a room-specific upgrade

Use a dehumidifier when excess moisture and condensation are the problem. Use an air purifier when airborne particles are the priority. They solve different problems and neither replaces ventilation or repairs.

A realistic starter setup

For most people, one well-fitting chair, a correctly positioned monitor and regular movement breaks matter more than decorative accessories. Add the standing desk only when the budget allows and the room can accommodate it.
