How the calculation works

Cost = watts ÷ 1,000 × hours × electricity rate. The calculator assumes the stated wattage is drawn continuously. Real compressor appliances cycle as conditions change, so actual cost may be lower or higher depending on temperature, humidity and mode.

Finding the correct numbers

  • Use rated or measured wattage from the exact model
  • Use the unit rate from your electricity bill, excluding the standing charge
  • Estimate realistic runtime rather than entering 24 hours automatically
  • Recalculate when your tariff changes

Example for the Arete One 12L

The calculator starts at 151 W because that is the published figure used in our Arete One 12L research. At 24.5p/kWh for six hours, the simple maximum-draw estimate is about 22p. Cycling behaviour means this is an estimate, not a promise.
