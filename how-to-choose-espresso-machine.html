How to Choose a Home Espresso Machine

The right machine depends less on the longest feature list and more on the routine you will enjoy every morning.

Updated 24 September 2026·Practical UK guide
Quick answer

Choose by the amount of control you want: capsules for convenience, automatic bean-to-cup for one-touch drinks, or a manual/semi-automatic machine for learning and control.

Start with the workflow

Manual espresso involves grinding, dosing, tamping, extracting and steaming. That can be satisfying or frustrating. Be honest about whether you want a hobby, a fast drink or something between the two.

Integrated grinder or separate grinder?

An integrated grinder saves counter space and gives beginners one coordinated package. Separate grinders offer more upgrade choice and can improve consistency, but increase cost and complexity.

Understand the real budget

Include fresh beans, scales, filters, cleaning products and possibly a better tamper or milk jug. A machine only saves money against coffee shops if you use it consistently.

  • Allow room above the machine for the hopper
  • Check water hardness and filtration needs
  • Choose readily available cleaning supplies

Milk drinks need practice

A manual steam wand can create café-style texture, but technique matters. Automatic milk systems are easier and more repeatable, while giving less control.

Who suits the Barista Express?

It suits a beginner who wants to learn without immediately buying a separate grinder. It is less suitable for someone who expects one-button coffee or plans to upgrade individual components quickly.
